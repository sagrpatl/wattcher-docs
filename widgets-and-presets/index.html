Widgets and presets

Guide › Widgets and presets

Besides the main card, Wattcher has eight widgets: small overlays you can place anywhere on the screen. Like the main card they let clicks through, and like the main card they follow the ride live. Turn them on and off in Control window → Overlay (or the phone’s Overlay tab), or let a preset pick them.

The eight widgets

Section titled “The eight widgets”

Intervals & splits: the last two intervals (target, your average and a rating), the current one (average so far, time left) and the next three. The rating, for ERG intervals: ★ you were within ±5 % of the target for at least 80 % of the interval, ☆ at least half of it, · less. Its options: the Time column shows time left, % done of the current interval, or Bar only (no numbers), and the Target, Your average, On-target mark and Time / % columns can each be switched off. Both are part of the preset.

Workout strip: the workout’s profile along the bottom of the screen with your power and a playhead: the next 10 minutes (and the last 2), or the whole workout (Overlay tab → Workout strip → Shows). Beside it: your ride time so far, what’s next and the time left in this interval. Without a workout it shows your last 10 minutes of power.

Big number: one number, large: 3-second power, interval time left, target, heart rate, cadence or % of FTP (Overlay tab → Big number → Shows). Power and % of FTP turn green / amber / red for on / under / over target.

Zone time: time and share in power zones Z1–Z6 this ride (pauses don’t count).

Workout progress: elapsed, remaining, % done, and work so far against the workout’s estimate (kJ).

Clock: time of day and ride time.

Cadence: the main card’s cadence lane as its own, larger card: a 40–120 rpm gauge with your settled cadence (purple tick), the shaded “noticeably slower” band below it, the red floor under 62 rpm, and your cadence as a marker coloured like the cadence cues (white fine, amber notice, red spin up). The readout is “now / settled”, e.g. 84 / 90. Outside a ride it shows just your live cadence (if a sensor sends it).

Power: your 3-second power on a gauge centred on the target (it spans ±30 % of the target, at least ±40 W; beyond that the marker sits at the end with an arrow). The green band is the on-target band of the off-target cues (±8 %, at least 8 W, or what you set), and the marker is green / amber / red like the main card’s power badge. The readout is “now / target”, e.g. 212 / 220. In ERG the marker just sits on target. Without a target (no ride, a free-ride block) it shows only your live power.

Both gauges can be Vertical (the default) or Horizontal (laid on their side, low values on the left, the readout above): Overlay tab → Cadence / Power → Gauge, or the phone’s Overlay tab. The choice is part of the preset, like the big number’s metric.

Missing sensors show “—”, never 0. A paused ride dims the widgets with a PAUSED tag.

The Overlay tab

Section titled “The Overlay tab”

For each overlay (the main card too): an on / off switch, its own size (− / +, 50–200 %), its options (main card layout and interval clock, big number metric, strip mode, gauge orientation, the splits’ time column and columns), and Place (snap grid, nudge arrows, Reset position). Arrange on screen (Ctrl+Shift+H) lets you drag them all. Moving and sizing work as described in The overlay.

Presets

Section titled “Presets”

A preset is a whole overlay set: which overlays are on, the main card’s layout, where each one sits and how big it is.

Preset Main card Widgets
Minimal Pill Workout strip
Training Compact Workout strip, Intervals & splits
Big screen hidden Big number (140 %), Workout strip, Clock
Everything Compact all eight (Cadence and Power vertical)

  • Switch in the Overlay tab, with Ctrl+Shift+P (also mid-ride), the menu-bar icon’s Next overlay preset, or the phone (Overlay tab, or the preset button next to “Hold to end workout” on the Ride tab). A short note shows the new preset’s name.
  • Moving or resizing keeps the preset: it remembers where you put things, per preset.
  • Turning overlays on or off, changing the main layout, the big number, a gauge’s orientation or the look makes a Custom set (based on the preset you started from).
  • Reset sizes puts every overlay back to the preset’s size; Reset preset puts back its layout, spots and sizes.

With the main card hidden (Big screen), interval summaries, Get ready, Undo after a skip, preset changes and sensor messages appear in a small toast strip at the top center of the screen, only while there is something to show.

Themes and card style

Section titled “Themes and card style”

Overlay tab → Look (or the phone’s Overlay tab) changes how every overlay looks:

Theme What it’s for
Dark glass The usual look: dark see-through cards.
High contrast Solid black cards, bright text and colours. For busy or bright games.
Minimal outline Almost no card, outlined numbers. Barely there over video.
Colour-safe Colours that work with red–green colour blindness; the power badge, big number and Power gauge also show ✓ on target, ▼ under, ▲ over.
Bold Bigger numbers, solid blue cards, a thick zone stripe.

On top of the theme you can set the cards’ opacity, blur (frosted glass) and corners, and pick an accent colour (the progress bar, highlights, toasts). Reset to the theme’s style goes back to the theme’s own. Blur costs GPU time while a game runs; low opacity and Minimal outline cost nothing.

The look belongs to the preset, like the big number: the built-in presets are Dark glass, changing the look makes a Custom set, and a saved preset keeps its own look. So a “TV” preset can be Bold while “Training” stays glass.

Your own presets

Section titled “Your own presets”

  • Save as preset… keeps what’s on screen (overlays, layout, positions, sizes, look, strip mode) under a name. Up to 20.
  • Saved presets are listed after the built-in ones. While one is active, moving or sizing things updates it. Update saves the current layout into it; Rename and Delete do what they say.
  • In cycle: each preset has a switch for whether Ctrl+Shift+P (and Next preset) goes through it. Custom is never in the cycle. Example: leave only “Training” and your own “TV” in the cycle, and Ctrl+Shift+P flips between the two.

When mirroring, each computer keeps its own overlay set: a Big screen layout on the TV, Training on the laptop.

Tip: arrange with Preview running, so every widget shows live numbers.

Switch presets by app

Section titled “Switch presets by app”

Wattcher can change the preset for you when you change apps: Big screen over the game, Minimal over a video, Training otherwise. Set it up in Control window → Overlay → Switch by app:

  1. Turn on Switch presets by the app in front.
  2. Bring the app (the game, the video app) to the front for a moment, then come back to Wattcher.
  3. Add an app… lists the apps you used recently. Click Add, then pick its preset.
  4. Any other app decides what happens in every other app: Keep the current preset (the default) or a preset of your choice.

How it behaves:

  • The preset changes once an app has stayed in front for about 1.5 seconds, so a quick Alt-Tab (⌘Tab) changes nothing. It works with or without a ride.
  • Wattcher’s own windows, the Dock, Spotlight, notifications and the lock screen don’t count as an app.
  • If you pick a preset yourself (Overlay tab, Ctrl+Shift+P, the tray or the phone), your choice holds until you switch to another app.
  • The first rule for an app wins. Use ↑ / ↓ to change the order, Remove to delete a rule. A rule whose preset you deleted shows Deleted preset and is skipped until you pick another one.
  • Windows games under CrossOver are told apart by their .exe (shown as “Windows game”). On Linux this needs KDE Plasma; Steam games show up as “Steam game <number>”.
  • Each switch is written to the app log with its reason (Settings → Advanced → Show logs).
  • The phone’s Overlay tab has the same on / off switch. Rules are set up on the computer, and each computer keeps its own (mirroring doesn’t copy them).

Nothing extra is needed: no Screen Recording or Accessibility permission on a Mac.
