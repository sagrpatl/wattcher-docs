Your first ride

Guide › Your first ride

1. Pick a workout

Section titled “1. Pick a workout”

Control window → Library. Workouts come from your Workouts folder (~/Documents/TrainerOverlay/Workouts): .zwo (Zwift), .erg and .mrc (TrainerRoad and others) and intervals.icu text (.txt) files. Import workout… copies a file in; Open workouts folder shows the folder; the Builder writes new ones.

Click a workout to select it, or press its Start to ride it right away. Edit opens it in the Builder.

2. Choose who controls the trainer

Section titled “2. Choose who controls the trainer”

Settings → Riding sets how every ride starts. The Ride tab (and the phone’s More tab) switch just the current ride; the next ride starts the Settings way again.

  • Wattcher controls the trainer: Wattcher sets the resistance for every interval in ERG mode. The trainer holds the target watts whatever your cadence.
  • Just show my data: another app such as Zwift or SYSTM controls the trainer. Wattcher only shows your numbers and the workout’s targets as a guide, and still records the ride.

You can switch any time, also mid-ride. In Just show my data the overlay’s ERG button is off and the card shows NO ERG; a ride that starts that way says “ERG is off” with a Use ERG button. Pressing ERG (or Ctrl+Shift+G) hands the trainer back to Wattcher for that ride.

3. Start

Section titled “3. Start”

Press Start (Ride tab, or the workout’s Start in the Library). The overlay shows the workout; the clock runs as soon as you pedal.

Not set up a trainer yet? Start anyway: the ride runs along with no trainer (useful to try things; the trainer can join later).

4. During the ride

Section titled “4. During the ride”

Hover the overlay’s card to show its controls (the rest of the overlay lets clicks through to the video below):

Button Does Shortcut
⏮ Back Restart this interval; press again for the previous one Ctrl+Shift+Left
⏸ Pause / ▶ Resume Ctrl+Shift+Space
⏭ Skip Next interval (Undo shows for 5 s) Ctrl+Shift+Right
− / + Easier / harder by 1 % (Shift-click: 5 %); with no ERG target (free ride, ERG off), shift a virtual gear (Shift-click: 3) Ctrl+Shift+Down / Up
+30s Add 30 seconds to this interval Ctrl+Shift+E
ERG ERG on / off: off, you shift gears and make the power Ctrl+Shift+G
■ End Press and hold 1 s to end the workout (a quick click asks “End ride?” instead)

Harder / easier changes every target of the workout by default; Settings → Riding → “Harder / easier applies to” → Work intervals only limits it to the work intervals.

Make the gears feel real. Settings → Gears → Calibrate takes about a minute: spin up to 90 rpm, then hold that cadence while the resistance rises; it stops by itself at your FTP (stop earlier if it gets too hard). Wattcher measures the power each resistance level takes, and from then on each gear takes the power a real bike needs in that gear, one shift about one cog. In the same section:

  • Virtual gearing: the groupset whose 24 steps you shift through (a road 50/34 × 11–28 by default).
  • Your gear on the trainer: the chainring and cog the bike sits in (34 × 14 by default; a Zwift Cog is 14). Rides start in the virtual gear closest to it.
  • Road: Flat (big jumps in the big gears, good for sprints), Rolling or Climbing (every step harder).

Calibrate again after changing trainers; Forget goes back to plain resistance steps.

What Wattcher does for you:

  • Sound cues: 3-2-1 ticks before each interval change, then a start tone (higher when the next interval is harder, lower when easier), and a soft double beep before a big jump. Settings → Sounds; Ctrl+Shift+M mutes.
  • Auto-pause when you stop pedaling: the workout clock waits and resumes when you pedal again.
  • Sums up every sprint (max-effort or sprint step): average and peak power, kJ and cadence. Each one teaches Wattcher your sprint (where your power peaks, how fast you fade), which - 10s sprint steps use to set the resistance (see the workout text format).
  • Brakes the flywheel after a sprint: when an ERG step follows a free / max-effort step and the flywheel is still spinning much faster than your pedalling, the trainer brakes for a second or two (harder the faster it spins, at most 40 %) so you can pedal into the ERG target sooner, then eases into the target. It lets go as soon as you push (Settings → Advanced to turn it off).
  • Eases off when your cadence drops very low (below 55 rpm for 3 s, Settings → Riding), so you can spin back up instead of grinding to a halt.
  • A grace period of 10 s after Start and after every pause (no auto-pause, no easing off), and after a pause the target ramps back up over a few seconds instead of jumping (Settings → Advanced).
  • Off-target cues: a quiet badge, the cadence lane and the odd short cue such as “Spin up now”, and a scorecard after each work interval. See Off-target cues.
  • The display stays awake while you ride.

5. The end

Section titled “5. The end”

When the workout finishes, the trainer is released and the ride is saved as a .fit file. The overlay shows a short summary; the Control window’s Rides tab has the details (see Rides and .FIT files).

To stop early: hold the overlay’s End button or Hold to end on the Ride tab for 1 s (with the keyboard: hold Space or Enter on it; a quick click or key press asks “End ride? Yes / No” instead), or quit (Ctrl+Shift+Q also saves the ride). A ride left paused for 15 minutes ends by itself and releases the trainer. Rides shorter than a minute are thrown away unless Settings → Recording → “Keep rides shorter than 1 minute” is on.
